## Problem
Information about the territory, from its land and water to its ecosystems and the places that matter to the community, was spread across separate datasets, reports, and people's knowledge. There was no single place to see how it all fits together.

## Approach
I brought together field-collected data, LiDAR-derived elevation models, and public datasets, cleaned and aligned them, and designed a series of thematic maps covering the territory, from watersheds to individual sites. Each map is paired with a written narrative, so the atlas reads as a story about the territory rather than a set of figures.

Following OCAP principles, the community decides what goes in the atlas and who it is shared with, and sensitive locations are generalized or left out.

## Outcome
A print-ready atlas the community can use for territorial planning, funding proposals, and teaching, built on a consistent set of map layers they can keep adding to.
