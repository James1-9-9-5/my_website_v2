title: Beyond the Map
summary: How I found my way into GIS, what I do outside work, and why people are the thread through all of it.

    image: ../../media-source/beyond-the-map/photo4.jpg
    alt: James sitting in the forest at Parque Machía in Bolivia with a coati resting on his lap.
    caption: And smile... ft. Christina.
    paragraphs:
      - "To be honest, I stumbled into GIS. I first encountered it during a module in my Geography degree at Brighton, but at the time, I never imagined it turning into a career. After university, I spent a few years exploring different jobs, looking for the right fit."
      - "A little lost, I decided to travel. In 2019, I headed to Bolivia, where I spent time volunteering at Parque Machía caring for rescued animals, helping keep the sanctuary running, and occasionally ending up with a coati on my lap. While there, I saw researchers using spatial data to map the local forest and track deforestation. It was here that I re-encountered GIS, though it still did not occur to me that I too could pivot to this line of work."
  - side: left
    image: ../../media-source/beyond-the-map/photo2.jpg
    alt: Three people working together at laptops and monitors showing GIS maps in a field office on a game reserve in South Africa.
    caption: Working hard.
    paragraphs:
      - "When COVID hit a year later and gave me time to pause and reflect, GIS kept coming back to me. Remembering the work in Bolivia, I packed up and headed to South Africa for a GIS internship working alongside an Anti-Poaching Unit on a game reserve. Using spatial data to protect wildlife was the perfect bridge between my love for the natural world and a proper reintroduction to the realm of GIS. Here, I saw once again how geospatial work creates a real, tangible difference on the ground."
  - side: right
    image: ../../media-source/beyond-the-map/photo3.jpg
    alt: Bouldering up a large moss-topped boulder in a forest, with a friend watching from the crash pad below.
    caption: Reaching new heights.
    paragraphs:
      - "Outside of work, you'll usually find me outdoors, hiking, bouldering, or at the gym balancing out the hours spent behind a screen. Bouldering is my favourite because it's fundamentally about problem-solving, working through a route one move at a time, failing a few times, and feeling that satisfaction when you finally solve it. That same iterative mindset carries directly into how I approach complex GIS challenges."
      - "My love for travel remains insatiable, and has proved to be equally defining. Living and working in South Africa, Bolivia, and now Canada has taught me to embrace new environments, listen to diverse perspectives, and stay adaptable, qualities that are essential when collaborating with different communities today."
  - side: left

    paragraphs:
      - "None of this journey would mean as much, however, without my family and friends keeping me grounded. Moving between countries has made me treasure time spent with them even more, and for me, the best days are simple: good food, great company, and fresh air."
  - side: right
    image: ../../media-source/beyond-the-map/photo5.jpeg

    paragraphs:
      - "Ultimately, people are the thread connecting everything I do. Whether it's rangers on a reserve in South Africa, conservation teams in Bolivia, or my former colleagues in London (pictured on my last day with them), the people you work with matter just as much as the spatial systems you build. Building genuine relationships and bringing warmth to the everyday makes every project better, and that's the spirit I bring to every team and community I work with today."
